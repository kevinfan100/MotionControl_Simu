\section*{S1 --- gain law}

The truth is not a member of this one-curve family. Declared truncation,
measured offline on $\bw-\bw_s \in [0.1,\,25]$ from
\texttt{calc\_correction\_functions}:

\begin{equation*}
b_{\mathrm{state}} - 1:\quad
13.05\,\%\ \mathrm{RMS},\quad 15.34\,\%\ \sup,\quad
\text{range } [+7.79,\ +15.34]\,\%,\quad \text{one-signed},\quad
\text{far field} \to +1/8
\end{equation*}

The far-field limit is exact: $c_{\perp} \to 1 + 9/(8\bar{h})$ gives
$b_{\mathrm{state}} \to 9/8$. The supremum sits at
$\bw-\bw_s \approx 1.19$, i.e.\ $\bar{h} \approx 2.19$, inside the canonical
oscillation band --- the run's most informative segment coincides with the
worst point of the truncation.

The exponent $2$ is $1+1/p$ at $p=1$; both asymptotic anchors give $p=1$, so it
is anchored rather than chosen. No $8/9$ factor is carried (user decision,
2026-08-12), which is why the declared truncation is $13\,\%$ and not $0.7\,\%$.

\section*{S3 --- what $\delta a$ is, and what the model claims about it}

$\delta a$ is not postulated; it is what makes the law-based propagation exact,
so its closed form follows by subtraction. Three properties follow from it
directly and are used in S8:

\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\delta a$ is proportional to the increment, hence exactly zero in a
hold.
\item[(ii)] $1/b_{\mathrm{state}} - 1 \in [-0.133,\,-0.072]$ is one-signed, but
$\delta a$ is NOT: $\mathrm{sign}(\delta a) = -\mathrm{sign}(\text{increment})$,
so it reverses with the direction of travel. Measured on the canonical
scenario, $\sum_k \delta a = +0.0680$ over the descent, $-0.0401$ over the
ascent, and EXACTLY ZERO over a closed oscillation cycle --- both
$\ba'_{\mathrm{true}}\mathrm{d}\bw$ and $(1-\ba_{\mathrm{true}})^{2}\mathrm{d}\bw$
are exact differentials in $\bw$. The whole disturbance is acquired on the
descent; a closed oscillation offers a constant-$\delta a$ model nothing to
lock onto.
\item[(iii)] $(1-\ba_w)^{2}$ spans $0.83 \to 0.0019$, so $\delta a$ varies over
three decades.
\end{itemize}

$\delta a[k+1] = \delta a[k]$ is therefore a modelling CLAIM, not a consequence.

DECLARED, NOT DERIVED: the step from the differential law of S1 to the
difference equation of S4 is forward Euler and is not stated anywhere in the
main file. In the height writing that step is an error on top of a correct
model; here $\ba'$ reads the state, so the discretisation IS the model and the
estimator is exactly estimating a slightly different family. The flow is
autonomous, so an exact one-step map exists in closed form; adopting it would
remove the quadrature error identically. Deferred by user decision.

\section*{S6 --- the disturbance column, and a path the main file does not name}

The disturbance column of $F_e$ is the constant $1$: unlike a multiplicative
parameter it does not vanish at a turning point, in a hold, or in the far
field. $F_e(3,5) = 0$ because the control law is untouched by $\delta a$.
Separability: a constant $\delta a$ drives $e_{\ba_w}$ as a ramp whereas an
error in the gain state is a persistent offset.

$y_1$ ALSO CORRECTS THE GAIN. This is implied by $K = PH^{\top}S^{-1}$ with
$H_1 = \mathbf{e}_1^{\top}$, giving $K_1(4) = P(4,1)/S_1$, and $P(4,1)$ is built
by $F_e(3,4) = -F_{dw}$ and by $Q_{34}$. Nothing in the main file is wrong, but
the naming (``position measurement'' / ``gain readout'') has misled every
reader including the author. Measured on the canonical scenario:
$K_1(4)$ and $|P(4,1)|$ each rise by a factor $\approx 160$ at motion onset
(medians $1.93\times10^{-3} \to 3.12\times10^{-1}$ and
$1.00\times10^{-7} \to 1.57\times10^{-5}$), while $K_2(4) \approx 7\times10^{-4}$
--- three orders of magnitude smaller. A four-arm nested ablation over 8 seeds:
removing $y_2$ does not reduce the across-seed spread; removing $y_1$'s gain
correction drops it $34\times$; removing the MA(2) memory changes almost
nothing. The spread is motion-triggered, not time-accumulated (a $4\times$
longer initial hold adds $11\,\%$ to the pre-motion spread).

That path is CORRECTLY PRICED: $K_1(4)$ is within $0.04$--$16\,\%$ of the
realised optimum $E[e\,\nu]/E[\nu^{2}]$. The spread is therefore the
information cost of inferring gain from position error, not a defect; removing
the path costs $13.6$ points of hold level.

\section*{S8 --- what the constant claim omits}

$Q_{55}^{\ast}$ is computable from the S3 closed form, so what the claim omits
is priced rather than assumed. Because $\delta a \propto$ increment,
$Q_{55}^{\ast}$ is zero in a hold and grows with commanded speed: a
time-invariant container would be the wrong category even at the right
magnitude.

FIRST ARM: $Q_{55} = 0$. That is the claim ``no stochastic driving of
$\delta a$'', not the claim that $\delta a$ is constant, which S3(ii) already
refutes. Pre-registered consequence: $P_{55}$ decreases monotonically and
$\delta\hat{a}$ freezes late in the run; and since the true $\delta a$ vanishes
in a hold while the model keeps injecting $\delta\hat{a}$, the final hold should
show a spurious ramp of order the descent accumulation.

OPEN ITEM: $\delta a$ inherits the frequency content of the increment, so on a
sinusoidal trajectory it is dominated by the commanded frequency. The internal
model principle then says a constant model cannot reject it asymptotically; the
derivable alternative is a two-state oscillator at the known commanded
frequency. Not implemented.

The wall-position oracle is worth $2.1\times$ on its own (tier-1 versus

The true-slope arm improves $8/8$ and is statistically indistinguishable from
production tier-2 ($t = -0.48$), and better on the descent peak. Hence the
derivation, the implementation and the idea of integrating the gain are all
sound: the entire gap is that the integrand carries the state's own error,
amplified by $\partial\ba'/\partial\ba = -2(1-\ba)$. Production also
integrates --- its predict is $x_4 + \ba'(\cdot)$ exactly as here --- so it does
not re-anchor; the only structural difference is where $\ba'$ is evaluated.

The additive constant disturbance is falsified in four configurations: harmful
on canonical with either slope source, harmful on the Menq ramp
($\bar{h}\,6.67 \to 1.111$ over $10$ s, near-wall gate opened), and $\delta\hat{a}$
never leaves noise ($-5.4\times10^{-8} \pm 5.3\times10^{-6}$ against a prior of

Breaking the feedback by lagging the slope does not work: evaluating $\ba'$ at
the pre-update state changes nothing ($+0.076$, $t = 0.65$) and smoothing is
monotonically harmful. So the gap is the BIAS in $\hat{\ba}_w$, not the noise on

Menq's forgetting factor (thesis eq.\ 4.15,

two trajectories, two arms and $\lambda_f \in \{0.9999, 0.999, 0.99\}$. It does

over-pessimism further down: $\rho(t)$ here is not uniformly biased, and a
uniform-in-time inflation cannot help a deficit whose sign changes with time.


\section*{The anchor, and where the law's error actually lives (2026-08-13)}

$b_{\mathrm{state}} \to 9/8$ is exact in the far field, so $b = 9/8$ is the
asymptotic ANCHOR and $b = 1$ is the unanchored form. Both the slope and the
seed must carry it: $\int (1-\ba)^{2}/b\,\mathrm{d}\bw$ integrates to
$\ba = 1 - b/(\bw-\bw_0)$, and at $b = 9/8$, $\bw_0 = 0$ that IS the far-field
truth. The seed carried $b = 1$ regardless of the slope until this was fixed
(the fix moved the numbers by $< 0.06$ points; the arms are now consistent).

Four arms, 8 paired seeds, overall relative gain-error RMS:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{array}{lcccc}
 & \text{desc peak} & \text{osc RMS} & \text{overall} & \text{hold bias} \pm \text{across-seed sd} \\[0.2em]
\text{law at TRUE }\ba,\ b=1     & 12.96\pm2.22 & 6.24\pm1.59 & 8.26\pm1.69 & -12.17 \pm 2.32 \\
\text{law at TRUE }\ba,\ b=9/8   & 3.83\pm1.14  & 1.88\pm1.17 & 2.48\pm1.29 & -3.42 \pm 2.39 \\
\text{law at EST. }\ba,\ b=1     & 15.36\pm6.24 & 4.26\pm1.76 & 4.94\pm2.71 & +0.73 \pm 6.88 \\
\text{law at EST. }\ba,\ b=9/8   & 15.09\pm5.69 & 4.17\pm2.32 & 5.12\pm3.32 & +3.33 \pm 7.12
\end{array}
\end{equation*}

Paired: exogenous $-5.785 \pm 0.587$, $t = -27.9$, better $8/8$. State-fed
$+0.180 \pm 1.366$, $t = +0.37$, better $3/8$. The paired sd bounds the
closed-loop effect at roughly $[-0.8, +1.1]$ points, so it is at least $5\times$
smaller than the open-loop effect --- an interval, not an absence of evidence.

THE DISSOCIATION IS THE RESULT. The law's error lives ENTIRELY in the bias:
anchoring removes $72\,\%$ of it ($-12.17 \to -3.42$) and leaves the across-seed
scatter untouched ($2.32 \to 2.39$). Feeding the law the state instead removes
the bias already ($+0.73$) and pays $3\times$ the scatter ($6.88$). So there is
nothing left for the anchor to fix in the closed loop, and $4.94\,\%$ is scatter
rather than bias.

Hence the paradox that opened this: feeding the law a PERFECT argument makes it
WORSE ($8.26$ against $4.94$). The state-fed integrand does not track the law;
it tracks the measurements, which are unbiased and noisy. The state writing's
own feedback is what discards the law.

Read off the figures (\texttt{formC\_dist\_cmp\_anchor\_exo\_*} against
\texttt{\_anchor\_state\_*}): the exogenous arms' error is a SMOOTH DETERMINISTIC
curve with two valleys locked to the oscillation, reaching $-13\,\%$ and settling
there, and the anchor scales the whole curve down by $\approx 3\times$. The
state-fed arms' error is ragged noise about zero with no valleys, and the two
columns are visually indistinguishable.

By this project's own no-trade-off rule, a bias-versus-scatter trade set by ONE
choice --- where $\ba'$ is evaluated --- means the writing is wrong rather than
that a compromise must be struck. Production evaluates at the COMMANDED height
(\texttt{gap\_d = w\_bar\_d - law\_ws}), which is exact and free, and gets low
bias AND low scatter at $2.07\,\%$.
